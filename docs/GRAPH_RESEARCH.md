# Research behind sourced graph discovery

Adapted from the standalone Trurl research notes at commit `3e51e22a98195b5bd57614d19979af4b23b56375`; the original source-review dates below are retained. In Hermes, native tools keep authoritative state in the active profile’s SQLite database. References to profiles, feedback ledgers or JSON describe logical records and temporary internal snapshots, rather than files to create or edit.

Sources reviewed **3 November 2026**. This is a small, local retrieval and curation design informed by research. No study below evaluates this implementation, and no recommendation uplift, causal explanation of taste, calibrated confidence, or bandit guarantee is claimed. The [operating reference](../skills/trurl/references/knowledge-graph.md) and [state contract](../skills/trurl/references/state-contract.md) define its actual behavior.

## Evidence and the implementation boundary

| [W3C SKOS Reference](https://www.w3.org/TR/skos-reference/), Recommendation, 18 August 2009 | Concepts have labels and aliases, direct hierarchical links, and associative links. `skos:related` is not transitive; direct broader links are distinguished from transitive hierarchy queries. | Typed labels and explicit edge direction; no transitive association claims. The local JSON vocabulary is inspired by these distinctions, not an RDF/SKOS implementation or a claim of ontology equivalence. |
| [W3C PROV-O](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/), Recommendation, 30 April 2013 | Provenance can describe entities, activities, attribution, derivation, quotation, and revision rather than treating every statement as an independent original source. | Source IDs, assertion methods, checked dates, and preserved correction provenance. No OWL reasoner, PROV serialization, automatic source verification, or semantic truth guarantee. |
| Xian et al., [Reinforcement Knowledge Graph Reasoning for Explainable Recommendation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.05237), SIGIR 2019 | PGPR couples recommendation and explanation through actual graph paths, using reinforcement learning, action pruning, rewards, and multi-hop scoring; it was evaluated on benchmark datasets. | Keep the actual path available for inspection and bound traversal. No PGPR policy, embeddings, learned reward, user-item training, or transfer of its benchmark results. A visible path alone establishes neither fit nor causality. |
| Balloccu et al., [Reinforcement Recommendation Reasoning through Knowledge Graphs for Explanation Path Quality](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.04954), revised November 2022 | The authors distinguish product relevance from explanation-path properties including recency, popularity, and diversity, and evaluate optimization methods on three public datasets. | Review path usefulness separately from candidate scores; retain sources and audit anchor/hub concentration. No implementation of their path objectives, optimizer, or validated human-understandability metric. |
| Chaney, Stewart and Engelhardt, [How Algorithmic Confounding in Recommendation Systems Increases Homogeneity and Decreases Utility](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.11214), RecSys 2018 | Simulations show that treating recommendation-influenced behavior as ordinary training data can increase homogeneity without corresponding utility gains. This is simulation evidence, not a universal measured effect. | Separate recommendation exposure from user evidence; a graph frontier or delivered work cannot teach itself into the profile. No causal debiasing estimator or demonstrated prevention of all feedback loops. |
| Hu, Koren and Volinsky, [Collaborative Filtering for Implicit Feedback Datasets](https://yifanhu.net/PUB/cf.pdf), ICDM 2008 | Implicit actions are noisy: purchase may mean a gift, and non-consumption may mean unavailability. Frequency is distinct from preference strength. Their factor model explicitly separates preference and confidence. | Keep exposure, consumption, liking, and familiarity distinct. We do not implement their implicit-feedback matrix factorization, infer negative preference from missing actions, or use repeated actions as automatic personal evidence. |
| Parapar and Radlinski, [Diverse User Preference Elicitation with Multi-Armed Bandits](https://research.google/pubs/diverse-user-preference-elicitation-with-multi-armed-bandits/), WSDM 2021 | Their experiments connect biased elicitation to biased later recommendations and diversify elicited preferences using a bandit method, improving diversity and serendipity in the tested setting. | Broaden anchors and research directions before ranking; accept newly stated interests independently. Optional targeted questions remain a host workflow. No trained bandit, information-gain optimizer, reward estimates, or claim that seeded variation reproduces their method. |
| Li, Chu, Langford and Schapire, [A Contextual-Bandit Approach to Personalized News Article Recommendation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.0146), WWW 2010, revised 2012 | LinUCB learns from contextual article exposures and click feedback. Its offline replay evaluation relies on logged random traffic; its reported gains concern clicks in the studied news setting. | Record eligible pools, seeds, selection channels, and exposure so future experiments are inspectable. No LinUCB algorithm, confidence-bound learning, regret guarantee, click objective, or valid off-policy estimator from ordinary recommendation history. |
| Abdollahpouri et al., [The Impact of Popularity Bias on Fairness and Calibration in Recommendation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.05755), October 2019 | Experiments on MovieLens and Yahoo Movies associate greater popularity-bias amplification with greater recommendation miscalibration; users whose historical ratings favor less-popular items are particularly affected. Calibration here concerns the representation of rated-item categories, not truth or enjoyment probabilities. | Protect minor evidenced interests from being crowded out and audit their representation. No trained calibration optimizer, fairness guarantee, demographic inference, or adoption of historical category proportions as a fixed target. |

## Design consequences

Maintain a sourced world graph and a separate personal evidence model. World nodes describe topics, works, creators, and qualities. Personal interest and familiarity remain in the profile and feedback ledger with their own provenance. A creator or topic adjacent to a liked work is a candidate to research, not a newly learned preference. Unmatched user interests remain valid: graph coverage must not limit what the person may become interested in.

The frontier follows only verified, sourced edges, retains actual node/edge IDs and traversal direction, and limits hops and neighbors. It excludes hypotheses and retractions from retrieval. Round-robin anchor coverage, cycle prevention, destination deduplication, and the confidence floor are simple safeguards. The default two hops, maximum three, confidence floor of 0.5, and bounded open allowance are engineering choices to test, not findings established by the cited papers. The path's weakest-edge annotation is not the probability that the whole path is true or that the person will enjoy the destination.

Graph frontier traversal is read-only and does no networking or semantic verification. The native `trurl_graph` tool also persists explicitly reviewed graph updates through the profile’s SQLite bridge. Validation checks signatures, identifiers, required provenance, and ranges. A structurally valid source can still be mistaken, stale, or misleading. The researching host must check the relevant source before claiming a useful factual bridge or recommending a work. Fictional public fixtures demonstrate mechanics only.

Connected surprise remains grounded in personal evidence. Open exploration requires an explicit request or a standing opt-in, and its selected destinations retain unknown personal fit. Explicit broad open mode reserves one qualified independent item so ranking cannot fill the entire slate with familiar items; if none qualifies, it reports an open shortfall. Standing permission alone does not require an open item. This reservation is an engineering choice, not a result established by these papers. An editorial registry and seeded category/field sampling offer reproducible opportunities conditional on a finite pool; they do not cover all knowledge or correct every popularity bias. The resulting research leads remain subject to verification, constraints, and a concrete value rationale. The implementation does not fabricate bridges or treat null relevance as a measured low preference.

Learn narrowly and preserve corrections. Explicit item feedback updates its actual target before any broader interpretation. A new interest may be recorded directly; it need not resemble old interests. A request for work research, a temporary mood, an illustration, quoted third-party words, or assistant-generated descriptions does not establish lasting taste. Familiarity needs its own evidence. Corrections retain the earlier record, source, scope, and before/after explanation while the latest explicit instruction governs. These are conservative workflow choices; no cited paper supplies a universal promotion threshold for a personal belief.

Protect known minor interests without freezing an incomplete starting profile. The calibration study compares historical rated-category distributions with recommendation distributions; those observations are not a complete map of what a person could enjoy. Here coverage audits should reveal neglect of evidenced interests while permitting independent new interests, corrections, and authorized open discovery. No fixed proportional taste target is implemented. Separately, edge confidence, subjective scores, model generation likelihood, and preference support are engineering annotations with different meanings; none is a calibrated probability of truth or enjoyment.

## Evaluation recommendations

Use anonymous, isolated fixtures and record the implementation revision, host/model, source access, research budget, prompts, retained plans, seeds, candidate pools, and failure counts. Keep verification and explicit constraints constant. Runtime tests establish execution properties; whole-agent evaluation is required to observe whether the host actually follows the evidence boundaries. Evaluation protocols remain specifications until an observed run is recorded.

1. **Integrity first.** Test unsupported relations, dangling nodes, fabricated or undated provenance, retracted/hypothesis exclusion, cycles, hubs, reverse direction, seed replay, route bounds, and unchanged input state. Check that a graph fact or exposure cannot create taste or expertise.
2. **Scope and permissions.** Test independently stated interests, illustrative examples, quoted content, temporary requests, sparse profiles, open opt-in, fixed priorities, contradictory feedback, hop-limit corrections, and safe operation without a graph. Verify that optional graph use never widens memory or past-chat access.
3. **Ablate the retrieval aid.** Compare the same synthetic profiles and requests with direct anchored discovery, the graph frontier, and explicitly authorized graph-plus-open exploration. Match effort and final count. Evaluate source-supported bridges, constraint compliance, duplicates, and preserved minor interests before comparing subjective fit or surprise.
4. **Audit coverage honestly.** Report anchor coverage, unmatched anchors, destination repetition, creator/topic concentration, route lengths, missing labels, and registry strata. These are descriptive properties of the supplied corpus, not psychological identity measures. More entropy or more hops is not automatically better.
5. **Evaluate learning prospectively.** With optional human feedback, separately assess fit, useful surprise, familiarity errors, explanation accuracy, and the effort required to correct a belief. Do not use clicks, completion, silence, or the system's own scores as ground-truth enjoyment. Compare learned behavior with a fixed-baseline condition while preserving user control.
6. **Reserve statistical claims for suitable data.** Calibration needs defined outcomes and held-out observations; bandit/off-policy evaluation needs an appropriate logging policy and support. Do not substitute an LLM's reported generation probability for a known exposure probability. Do not claim causal or long-term benefits from synthetic scenarios alone.

The Hermes README’s native Atlas screenshots use a synthetic profile; their [capture metadata](../assets/screenshots/capture-metadata.json) records its scope. Automated tests also use isolated synthetic state. These fixtures contain no evidence about a real person’s taste and must not be imported into a profile as learned preferences.
